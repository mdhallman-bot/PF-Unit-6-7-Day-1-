Discover before naming

B. Working in an office job you do not particularly enjoy for $120,000 per year

Which would you choose?
Now name the idea: psychic incomeThe non-money satisfaction or value a person receives from work or an activity — such as enjoyment, purpose, status, flexibility, or pride. is the non-money value you get from a choice. A higher paycheck can matter a lot, but it is not the only kind of value.

6. Digital Pathway Sort

Self-checking · no AI
Sort each situation by the primary preparation route being described. Use what the scenario says — not assumptions about the occupation.
Examples: Paid work + classroom training = electrician apprenticeship · Short focused training / credential = welding certificate · College degree route = accounting degree · Employer / on-the-job training = a bank training a newly hired teller.
Names for patterns you just sorted:

ApprenticeshipA structured program combining paid, supervised work with related instruction. — paid work plus structured training.

CertificateA document showing completion of a focused education or training program. — completion of a focused program.

CredentialEvidence that a person has met a defined standard of knowledge or skill. Certifications and licenses are examples. — proof of a qualification or skill standard.

DegreeAn academic award earned after completing a college program, such as an associate or bachelor's degree. — an academic qualification from a college program.

LicenseLegal permission from a government authority to practice certain occupations or activities. — legal authorization to do certain work.

Employer trainingTraining provided by an employer after or as part of being hired. — skills learned through company-provided instruction and supervised work.

Important: these can overlap. A career may require a degree and a license, or training and an industry credential.

8. Opportunity Cost + Intro to ROI

Name the tradeoff

Opportunity costThe value of the next-best alternative you give up when you make a choice. asks: What am I giving up by choosing this?

Return on investment (ROI)A way to compare what you put into a choice with the financial value or benefits you may get back. asks: What am I putting in, and what might I get back?

For this unit, ROI is one lens — not a rule that says “always choose the highest salary.” Time, cost, risk, job opportunities, and psychic income all matter.

ROI example: becoming a teacher.
Maya wants to become a teacher. She could attend a private university and finish with about $120,000 in student-loan debt, or choose a lower-cost route that leads to the same required teaching license with much less debt. Either route could lead to a similar first teaching job and salary.

9. AI and the Future of Work

Complement or substitute?

AI exposure does not automatically mean a job disappears. Jobs are made up of many different tasks.

ComplementSomething that works with a person and makes the person's work more useful, productive, or effective. means AI helps a worker do part of the job faster or better.

SubstituteSomething that can take the place of a person for a particular task. means AI can take over a task that a person used to do.

People sometimes use the phrase “desktop jobs” for work done mostly on computers, but that is not a precise risk category. Current research finds clerical and highly digitized tasks tend to have greater exposure to generative AI, while most occupations are more likely to have some tasks changed than to disappear completely.
